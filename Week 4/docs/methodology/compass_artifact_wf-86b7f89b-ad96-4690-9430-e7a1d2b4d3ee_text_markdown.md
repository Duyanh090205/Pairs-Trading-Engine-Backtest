# Bull-Regime Failure Diagnosis: 1-Minute Cointegration Pairs

## 1. Root-cause verdict

**Primary mechanism: (a) factor-momentum dominance, manifesting at the spread level as (c) structural break in the cointegrating vector. Secondary: (b) dispersion compression, acting as a TC-amplifier, not a generator of directional drift. (d) Kalman over-tracking is ruled out on first-principles arithmetic.**

Quantifying (d) first, since it is the cheapest to dispose of. With $\delta=10^{-7}$, $Q=\delta R I_2$ on log-price 1-min bars: a $\sim$30%/yr vol equity has per-minute innovation variance $R\approx \sigma^2_{\min}\approx (0.30/\sqrt{252\cdot 390})^2 \approx 10^{-6}$. The state innovation variance per minute is $Q_\beta\approx 10^{-7}\cdot 10^{-6}=10^{-13}$. Over a 1-month trading window ($N\approx 8{,}200$ minutes) the cumulative random-walk variance on $\beta$ is $NQ_\beta\approx 8.2\cdot 10^{-10}$, i.e. $\sigma(\Delta\beta)\approx 3\cdot 10^{-5}$. That is six orders of magnitude below typical $\beta\sim O(1)$ and cannot translate into a systematic, slow spread drift large enough to drive Z=3.0 entries into 3-day-plus losing trades. This is consistent with Triantafyllopoulos & Montana (2011) and the Kinlay (2018) signal-to-noise sensitivity simulations, which show that with $Q/R\sim 10^{-7}$ the filter is essentially OLS-regularised; the Chan/Halls-Moore range where $\beta$ "wiggles" meaningfully is $\delta\in[10^{-4},10^{-3}]$. (d) is mathematically inconsistent with the observed time-scale of DRIFT and can be eliminated.

The DRIFT signature (slow post-entry trend, no jumps, no directional bias, 93% of losers) is the diagnostic fingerprint of factor-momentum overwhelming the idiosyncratic cointegrating residual. The pair-selection pipeline orthogonalises only against the *bilateral* relative-price relationship; it does not orthogonalise against latent style/sector/factor exposures that survive Johansen on 6-month formation when those factors were stationary in formation but trend in trading. In a bull regime with sustained momentum, growth, and low-vol factor returns (and in 2023–2024 a single-name AI thematic on top), a pair with even modest differential factor loading $\Delta b_f$ accumulates $\Delta b_f \cdot R^f_{[T,T+1m]}$ in the spread — a smooth, monotone *drift*, not a jump. This is the same pathology Avellaneda & Lee (2010) document when residual statistical arbitrage is run without explicit eigen/ETF factor neutralisation, and it is the mechanism Khandani & Lo (2007, 2011) invoke for the cross-sectional contrarian collapse: relative-value strategies fail when factor returns compound in one direction over horizons longer than the half-life. Mechanism (c) — Gregory-Hansen-style cointegrating-vector shift — is not an alternative; it is the *spread-space projection* of (a). Bull regimes produce asymmetric breaks because thematic dispersion is a directional event (one cohort revalues up, the complement does not), whereas bear regimes produce co-movement collapse to market beta, which mechanically preserves pairwise correlation (Khandani–Lo "everything correlates in a sell-off"; the same logic explains why Stübinger & Bredthauer 2017 and Krauss 2017 document the post-2010 secular HF-pairs decay concentrating in low-vol bull years).

The bear/bull Sharpe asymmetry (+1.357 vs −1.036) and the specific correlation collapse 0.35–0.40 → 0.10–0.15 are jointly explained only by (a)+(c). High realised vol in 2022 raised the OU crossing rate per unit time (more reversion events per trading month, less time for factor drift to accumulate before zero-cross exit); risk-off correlation regimes lifted within-pair correlation by raising the market-beta share of variance; cross-sectional dispersion was elevated, so 30 bps TC was a smaller fraction of mean-reversion amplitude. In 2023–2026, the inverse held: realised vol fell, pairwise correlation outside the market-beta channel collapsed because thematic factor returns dominated idiosyncratic noise, and OU half-life on the trading-window spread effectively lengthened past the trading horizon (formation half-life ≤ 6d is no longer informative of trading-window half-life when the long-run mean has shifted). Dispersion collapse (b) is real but is a TC amplifier and PnL compressor — it cannot by itself produce a −1.0 Sharpe with 93% drift-mechanic losers; it would produce noisy, near-zero PnL. (b) is a contributor to the magnitude, not the sign.

## 3. Recommended fix

**FIX D, implemented as a pre-trade persistence-of-correlation gate appended to the existing CORR25 filter.** Concretely: at the end of formation, partition the 6-month window into three contiguous 2-month blocks $B_1, B_2, B_3$ (on the 5-min log-return series used for the CORR25 computation). For each candidate pair surviving Johansen+BH-FDR+OU+last-month-persistence+CORR25, compute $\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3$ (Pearson on 5-min log returns, the same estimator already used for CORR25). Reject the pair if **either** (i) $\rho_3 < 0.15$ (final-block floor — anchored at the same magnitude as CORR25 to avoid introducing a new free scale), **or** (ii) $\rho_1 > \rho_2 > \rho_3$ **and** $\rho_1 - \rho_3 \geq 0.15$ (monotonic decay with material amplitude). The two-clause structure is deliberate: the floor catches end-of-formation collapse regardless of path; the monotonicity clause catches the slow-bleed pattern that the floor misses when $\rho_3 \approx 0.18$. Integration point is between CORR25 and Kalman initialisation, costing $O(N_{\text{pairs}})$ per fold — negligible relative to the existing 4-min/fold runtime. The gate is parameter-light: only the 0.15 amplitude threshold is tunable and it is anchored to CORR25's own scale, so it does not add an independent degree of freedom for PBO accounting.

## 4. Diagnostic test (pre-45-fold validation on the 23-fold dataset)

**Procedure.** On the 23 completed folds, label each *traded* pair (post-all-existing-gates) by its formation-window block-correlation profile: DECAY if it satisfies clause (i) or (ii) of FIX D above, STABLE otherwise. For each fold, compute fold-level mean per-trade PnL separately on DECAY and STABLE subsets. Stratify by regime: 2022 folds (bear, ~6) vs 2023+ folds (bull, ~17). The pre-registered statistic is the *bull-regime* difference $\Delta_{\text{bull}} = \overline{\text{PnL}}_{\text{STABLE,bull}} - \overline{\text{PnL}}_{\text{DECAY,bull}}$ in $\$$/trade, with a fold-clustered (cluster-robust) one-sided $t$-test, $H_0: \Delta_{\text{bull}} \leq 0$.

**Pass / fail thresholds.** *Pass* — STABLE PnL exceeds DECAY by an amount large enough that, after dropping DECAY pairs, the bull-regime fold-mean Sharpe crosses zero: with the current bull mean Sharpe of $-1.036$ and assuming DECAY captures ~25–35% of bull-regime trades, the implied required per-trade PnL gap is roughly $\Delta_{\text{bull}}\geq 0.6$–$0.9$ stop-loss-units, equivalent to a Sharpe-on-trades of about $\geq 0.4$ on the DECAY exclusion. Statistical bar: cluster-robust $p < 0.025$ (Bonferroni-corrected for the four candidate fixes, equivalent to BH-FDR at $q=0.10$ across A/B/C/D). *Falsifying outcome* — $|\Delta_{\text{bull}}| < 0.2$ Sharpe-on-trades or $p > 0.10$, in which case FIX D is rejected and the pivot is to FIX A (which targets the same root cause through the spread-residual channel rather than the return-correlation channel).

**Power realism.** Assuming ~40–60 trades per bull fold $\times$ 17 bull folds $\approx$ 700–1{,}000 bull trades, with DECAY share ~30%, a one-sided cluster-robust test (effective $n_{\text{eff}}$ ~17 fold-clusters) has ~70–80% power to detect a true per-trade Sharpe gap of 0.5 at $\alpha=0.025$. Power drops sharply for gaps below 0.3, which is fine — a smaller gap would not rescue the strategy economically and should fail the test. Sample size is the binding constraint: do **not** sub-stratify further (e.g. by sector or sub-period) on this dataset, since cell counts collapse below useful power and the multiple-testing budget has already been spent on the four-fix comparison.
